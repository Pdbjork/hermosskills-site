Maintainer economics for agent skills.

AI teams depend on small, trusted operational skills: deploy checklists, safety gates, model-provider setup, browser QA, Stripe go-live, and incident runbooks. The people maintaining those practices rarely get paid for the quiet work that keeps them usable.

Send a sponsor-fit questionSee the review method

The simple model

  • $49/mo sponsors maintenance of one public skill.
  • $250/mo supports the opening catalog and audit method.
  • $500 deposit scopes a custom skill, integration, or audit.
  • 30% public-good rebate from commission revenue is reserved for upstream OSS AI projects.

Why a maintainer or tool builder should care

Uncurated skill directories optimize for surface area. Hermosskills optimizes for trust: a visible review method, dated catalog entries, disclosure of sponsorship, and human stop points for risky work.

That creates a funding lane for the kind of maintenance work that usually disappears into Slack threads, README footnotes, and unpaid community support.

What sponsorship does not buy

  • No hidden placement or fake endorsement.
  • No promise that a skill is safe forever.
  • No unattended posting, emailing, deploying, payment movement, or private-data scraping.
  • No legal, tax, banking, or compliance advice.

What a sponsor gets before paying

  1. The skill category or public artifact to maintain first.
  2. The visible sponsor disclosure language.
  3. The review/audit path for that skill.
  4. The tier recommendation: $49/mo, $250/mo, or custom commission.
